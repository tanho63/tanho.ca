### croatia (split, plitvice lakes)

In August, I took the opportunity to do some Europe travels: my parents were
visiting my sister for about a month and a half, and my sister wanted to take
a few days off so that they could go on a trip somewhere in Europe. I decided that
joining would be fun, considering how burnt out I was feeling, and we settled on
a trip to Croatia. Landed in the middle of a heat wave (an arrival temperature
of 39C!) and got to see lots of Roman ruins, hiking, and some beach swimming. It
was a good trip overall, although in exchange for my demand that we take
air-conditioned siestas from 2pm - 5pm, we found ourselves packing in a lot of
early mornings and late nights, which was definitely tiring. It was also rather
strange to be travelling with the parents again - I've travelled a lot with Mi Yen
but not as much with Mom and Dad lately, and it's strange how much it feels like
the shoe is on the other foot in terms of parenting responsibilities. Mi Yen and I
spent a lot of time worrying about them: Mom was somewhat moody with Mimio for
most of the trip, and Dad was particularly glued to his phone and not really
present and taking in the sights and surroundings. It's rather a callback to when
I was glued to my GameBoy playing Pokemon as a kid on family trips.

### posit conf (houston, tx) and some AI reflections

PositConf has solidly become a "catching up with my R friends" thing instead of
a "learning new things in R", which is mostly because of my experience but a
little more uncomfortably because of the focus has shifted towards AI and
LLM-derived tooling. Where last year felt like it was 10% AI talks, this year
solidly felt like 50% of the talks involved AI in some way, shape, or form. I
really enjoyed Christine Zhang's keynote about NYT data viz, and Wes McKinney's
thoughts on AI were interesting, but my overall feelings coming out of the
conference are similar to my friend Jon Harmon: just broadly
sad about the AI shift.

{{< bluesky "https://bsky.app/profile/jonthegeek.com/post/3mvyhz6vlus2i" >}}

I think something that I internalized over this conf was that the people who
"offload" to AI for a given project care more about the **outcome** of that project
than they do about the **craft** of doing that project. I'm deeply invested in the
craft of so many different things that it feels like I avoid fully offloading to
AI on basically any topic. Even still, there are things I use AI for: sometimes
for generating art images, sometimes for naming things, sometimes for drudgery
in SQL optimizations, sometimes to interview me to help me do stuff I don't want
to do from scratch (writing peer reviews is hard!) - these are things I care more
about the outcome for - moreso than the craft itself. Other people's interests
are naturally different, so the things they are willing to offload to AI are
different: I've heard professors say they want all essays to be human written,
but in the same token they're willing to let LLMs derive the code for coding
assignments. I'm a programmer at heart, so I want code to be derived by a human,
but I could not be arsed to find pictures for slide decks I have to put together.
It's natural that these don't perfectly overlap for every human in the world, but
it also means that literally everyone ends up dealing with AI slop in their field,
and this sucks. Probably longer form thoughts are due here.

I also had a great time hanging out with my soccer engineering squad, which is
